I am a research assistant from the University of Michigan (UMSI) working with Dr. Michaelanne Thomas in the Anthropology and Technology Lab. I’ve examined how gendered norms shape participation in a Pakistani Facebook tech community and how online communities of trans people enact care and asymmetrically distribute infrastructural labor. I’m also a country research representative working on Project Provenance, an MSR funded study on how people assess, share or ignore provenance signals on social media.

New technologies arrive into existing social spheres. Across Human Computer Interaction (HCI), Computer-Supported Cooperative Work (CSCW) and Information Communication Technology and Development (ICTD), I am interested in studying how emerging technologies shape communities, and how those communities encounter them. I use qualitative methods, to gain deeper insight into lived experiences.

Currently, a UX Researcher @Philips. Previously, I was a graduate student @ UMSI, working as a teaching assistant for courses on online communities and usability evaluations. Even before that, I facilitated workshops with Indian handicraft communities on indigenous design in contemporary architecture.

Open to research collaborations, and to part-time teaching positions from January 2027.

research

  • "If we post, what will people think of us?": Offline Norms, Online Engagement, and Unpacking Gendered Experiences in a Pakistani Facebook Tech Community
    link

    Role: Research assistant and co-first author; worked with Hamza Naveed, Sheza Naveed, and Dr. Michaelanne Thomas

    We examined Tech Aids, a Pakistani peer-to-peer tech community on Facebook, through participant observation and interpretation of 20 semi-structured interviews with members. We uncovered how offline gender norms and patriarchal power structures constrain and shape participation within Tech Aids. We also proposed directions to support the participation and interaction of women members, drawing on situational privacy practices such as digital purdah and contextual integrity with a vulnerability-centric perspective. I led the framing of the paper, working on analysing themes, connecting findings and discussion to existing work, and addressing revise-and-resubmit feedback.

  • Compounding Online Infrastructural Labor: How Transgender Communities Infrastructure Technological Trans Care in Community-Built Online Communities.

    Role: Research assistant and second author (under review, 2026)

    Examining how trans people build and maintain online communities and the additional infrastructural labor TBIPOC people face. I assisted with axial coding and worked on synthesizing literature on infrastructural labor in precarious conditions. This study shaped how I think about the infrastructural labor and care work that keeps online spaces running, and who ends up doing a greater share of the work.

  • Project Provenance
    Ongoing

    Role: Country research representative, funded by Microsoft Research and the University of Colombo, Sri Lanka, alongside country representatives for a multi-country study within South Asia

    I’m recruiting participants through purposive sampling across age, gender, urbanicity, digital literacy, and occupation within local community networks, and interviewing them 1:1 about provenance signals and the strategies people use when encountering AI-generated content. Through conversations and think-aloud protocols, I’m observing their influences and reflecting on the consequences (and uses) of AI-generated content on social media platforms.

  • Challenges and design opportunities for online schooling in India
    link

    Role: Research assistant, Summer course 2020

    My first foray into qualitative research, where I conducted 1:1 semi-structured interviews with teachers, students, and parents to understand how schools adapted teaching and daily activities to video conferencing platforms during the COVID-19 pandemic. As part of a five-person team, my contributions included interpreting interviews, identifying themes that highlighted teachers’ difficulties, and proposing design directions for hybrid online-offline schooling systems.
